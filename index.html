01 / Research

Trust is a relationship.
Technology changes it.

My work examines how news organizations, audiences, and emerging intermediaries negotiate credibility and responsibility. These questions connect my journalism experience with a developing research agenda on authority and technological mediation.

01

Developing research direction

The human–AI boundary in news

How is trust constructed when “human” and “AI” no longer adequately describe the source of news?

Read the research direction

I am interested in how journalism explains AI’s role to audiences: who produced information, who verified it, and who takes responsibility. Textual and thematic analysis offer a way to study these claims in audience-facing disclosures and public explanations.

Focus: AI disclosure, responsibility, journalistic authority

02

Conference paper · AEJMC Midwinter, 2026

Examining the trust gap

How do journalists and audiences explain declining trust—and where do their expectations for repair diverge?

Read the project overview

This work examines journalists’ and audiences’ attributions of declining news media trust and relational repair in the United States. It asks how transparency, responsiveness, and dialogue fit into their understandings of a trustworthy news relationship.

Focus: trust attributions, journalist–audience relationships

03

Conference paper · AEJMC, 2025

Political influencers and authority

What happens to the authority of journalism when political information also comes from digital creators?

Read the project overview

My conference work analyzed political influencers’ framing of the 2024 U.S. election on X. This connects to a broader interest in how creators and platforms participate in the production of political information and claims to credibility.

Focus: political framing, platforms, changing sources of authority

03 / About

From the newsroom
to the research question.

I am a PhD student in Journalism Studies at the University of Maryland’s Philip Merrill College of Journalism. My research brings together media trust, journalistic authority, political communication, and AI- and platform-mediated communication.

Before graduate study in the United States, I worked in reporting and newsroom editing in Bangladesh. That experience informs the questions I ask about journalism’s public role, its responsibilities, and its relationship with audiences.
